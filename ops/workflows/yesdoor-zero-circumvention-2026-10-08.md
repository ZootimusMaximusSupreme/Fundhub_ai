# Yesdoor: zero circumvention from all parties (2026-10-08)

Chris (owner-set): "We just need to ensure there is zero circumnavigating, from all parties."
Research: one agent, 40 searches. Bracketed numbers point to the source list at the bottom.

**Bottom line**
- No published number was found for how much any single tactic cuts leakage in apartment locating or senior placement.
- What does exist:
  - The industry rules: register first, be the named referral source, flag duplicates fast.
  - Arizona's senior statute, which has proof of introduction and a 14-day admission report built in [1].
  - Two outside studies:
    - At most 1 in 83 matches went around a home-cleaning app [2].
    - Blocking an outside chat tool cut detected bypassing by 18.7% on a freelance site [3].
- The ranking below is judgment: effect × legal safety × cost. The cost column is the agent's estimate, not sourced.

**Published leakage rates:**
- At most 1 in 83 matches went around a home-cleaning app that charged a 20–40% commission [2].
- About 90% of matched freelance jobs went around the platform, per a survey Gu cites [3].
- More trust between the two sides can raise bypassing [51].
- Homejoy is the standard example of a platform that failed this way [3][52].

## Recommended stack (in build order)

1. **Register before reveal (row 1).** For seniors, the AZ disclosure and acknowledgment is the timestamp.
2. **One contract for buildings and communities (rows 2, 3, 6, 7).** It holds all of these:
   - first registered wins
   - a 6-month window for apartments and 12 months for senior
   - a 5-business-day duplicate flag
   - a 14-day self-report
   - audit rights
   - price parity
   - fee + interest + costs for an unreported placement
3. **Monthly reconciliation plus a yearly audit (row 3).**
4. **Renter move-in reward (row 4),** paid on a verified lease and disclosed. No family cash in FL (row 14).
5. **Extend the re-pull consent to move-in checks for 12 months (row 5).** Suggested wording (the agent's): "I authorize Yesdoor to get my credit report by soft inquiry every 30 days for 12 months, to update my matches and to confirm my address and whether I moved into a place Yesdoor showed me, so Yesdoor can collect its fee from that building or community." An address change triggers an audit request. Never NCOA.
6. **Broker partners (row 9):** tracking codes, 12-month protection, lease proof and a buyout fee.
7. **Staff (row 8):** a license-law clause, CRM roles with export logs, and pay only on reconciled placements. Covenants by state:
   - CA: confidentiality and trade secrets only.
   - FL: a non-solicit of 6 months or less (or the CHOICE Act for high earners).
   - AZ: a narrow non-solicit.
8. **Add-on partners and lenders (row 11):** data limited to named purposes, a 30–45-day window, no credit data.
9. **Written double-registration rule (row 10):** Caring.com's 5 days and NPRA's send-back-to-the-first-agent rule.
10. **In-app messaging that hides contact info (row 12).**

**What can't be stopped:** a renter or family who found the place on their own before Yesdoor, and a building that takes cash for a lease and leaves it off its rent roll. Both can only be caught, by audit or by the renter's reward claim.

**Not found:**
- The contract windows A Place for Mom, CarePatrol and Oasis use.
- Any agency dropping a community over unreported move-ins.
- Any A Place for Mom lawsuit to collect fees.
- Yardi, Entrata or RealPage documentation on locator lead-source fields.
- Whether using utility or move data is legal.
- Any measured leakage cut in these two industries.
