# Master reading list

Written 2026-10-04. Personal, not business. Live copy: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/22741a16-667a-4992-afe2-2385ca107bee

Read the phases in order and tick each book off when you finish it. Phase 1 comes first because body, focus, and follow-through feed everything after it. Read the first two books, act on them for 30 days, then move on.

## Phase 1: Execution, body, focus

- [ ] **Bigger Leaner Stronger**, Michael Matthews: the most practical lifting and diet plan for men.
- [ ] **Taking Charge of Adult ADHD**, Russell Barkley: timers, external reminders, and breaking work into small pieces.
- [ ] **The 12 Week Year**, Brian Moran and Michael Lennington: 12-week goals and a weekly scorecard.
- [ ] **The War of Art**, Steven Pressfield: the resistance that keeps you planning and talking when you should be doing.
- [ ] **Getting Things Done**, David Allen: every open task out of your head and onto a list.
- [ ] **Deep Work**, Cal Newport: holding long stretches of focus.
- [ ] **Atomic Habits**, James Clear: building habits through small steps. Reread.
- [ ] **Outlive**, Peter Attia: a practical plan for health and longevity.

## Phase 2: Maturity and self-command

- [ ] **King, Warrior, Magician, Lover**, Robert Moore and Douglas Gillette: the four mature masculine archetypes.
- [ ] **The Courage to Be Disliked**, Ichiro Kishimi and Fumitake Koga: dropping the need for approval.
- [ ] **How Emotions Are Made**, Lisa Feldman Barrett: your brain builds emotions, so you can learn to steer them.
- [ ] **The Power of Full Engagement**, Jim Loehr and Tony Schwartz: managing physical, emotional, and mental energy.
- [ ] **Learned Optimism**, Martin Seligman: training yourself into a positive mindset.
- [ ] **Meditations**, Marcus Aurelius (Gregory Hays translation): staying steady around irrational people.
- [ ] **Letters from a Stoic**, Seneca: practical Stoicism.
- [ ] **Man's Search for Meaning**, Viktor Frankl: self-command and meaning under the worst conditions.
- [ ] **Owning Your Own Shadow**, Robert A. Johnson: integrating the parts of you that you suppress.
- [ ] **12 Rules for Life**, Jordan Peterson: discipline, responsibility, and standing up for yourself.

## Phase 3: Attraction, women, conversation

- [ ] **Models**, Mark Manson: attraction built on honesty and having your own life together.
- [ ] **Impro**, Keith Johnstone: status in every interaction.
- [ ] **Dressing the Man**, Alan Flusser: fit, color, and proportion for your build.
- [ ] **The Evolution of Desire**, David Buss: the mate-preference research most dating books copy.
- [ ] **Mate**, Tucker Max and Geoffrey Miller: what women look for and how to build it.
- [ ] **Conversationally Speaking**, Alan Garner: starting and carrying conversations.
- [ ] **The Charisma Myth**, Olivia Fox Cabane: presence, power, and warmth.
- [ ] **What Every Body Is Saying**, Joe Navarro: reading body language.
- [ ] **Attached**, Amir Levine and Rachel Heller: attachment styles.
- [ ] **No More Mr. Nice Guy**, Robert Glover: stopping approval-seeking with women.
- [ ] **The Way of the Superior Man**, David Deida: masculine purpose and presence.
- [ ] **The Art of Seduction**, Robert Greene: the psychology of attraction.
- [ ] **The Rational Male**, Rollo Tomassi: frame and how women choose. Runs cynical.
- [ ] **Wired for Love**, Stan Tatkin: handling conflict before it spirals.
- [ ] **The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work**, John Gottman: which couples last and why.

## Phase 4: Warrior code

- [ ] **The Art of Learning**, Josh Waitzkin: how mastery actually happens.
- [ ] **The Book of Five Rings**, Miyamoto Musashi: strategy and discipline.
- [ ] **Bushido: The Soul of Japan**, Inazo Nitobe: the samurai code. Read before Hagakure.
- [ ] **Hagakure**, Yamamoto Tsunetomo: the raw samurai mindset on duty and death.
- [ ] **Musashi**, Eiji Yoshikawa: a novel that follows Musashi from a violent kid to a master.
- [ ] **Gates of Fire**, Steven Pressfield: the Spartans at Thermopylae.
- [ ] **Meditations on Violence**, Rory Miller: what real violence looks like.
- [ ] **On Combat**, Dave Grossman: the psychology of violence and trained fighters.

## Phase 5: Thinking and engineering

- [ ] **The Art of Doing Science and Engineering**, Richard Hamming: how to think, learn, and do great work.
- [ ] **The Systems Bible**, John Gall: why complex systems fail.
- [ ] **The Goal**, Eliyahu Goldratt: finding the one constraint that limits an operation.
- [ ] **A Philosophy of Software Design**, John Ousterhout: keeping code simple.
- [ ] **The Phoenix Project**, Gene Kim, Kevin Behr, and George Spafford: getting software to work end to end.
- [ ] **Designing Data-Intensive Applications**, Martin Kleppmann: databases and backends in depth. Read last in this phase.

## Phase 6: Human nature, the deep end

- [ ] **The Laws of Human Nature**, Robert Greene: why people act irrationally.
- [ ] **Games People Play**, Eric Berne: the hidden games people run.
- [ ] **Never Split the Difference**, Chris Voss: hostage negotiation tactics for sales and conflict.
- [ ] **Crucial Conversations**, Kerry Patterson and co-authors: high-stakes talks when emotions run hot.
- [ ] **Without Conscience**, Robert Hare: psychopathy.
- [ ] **Ordinary Men**, Christopher Browning: how ordinary police officers became mass killers.
- [ ] **Behave**, Robert Sapolsky: the biology behind behavior.
- [ ] **On the Genealogy of Morality**, Friedrich Nietzsche (Kaufmann translation): the original argument that pity is a weakness.
- [ ] **Crime and Punishment**, Fyodor Dostoevsky: a man who believes he's above ordinary morality.
- [ ] **The Denial of Death**, Ernest Becker: fear of death as the driver of behavior. Save for last.
