## HOUSE RULES SET WHILE WRITING THE STRAIGHT OFFER SET

- Open on the offer with the price in it. No wind-up before it.
- Say the thing. No "the full read", no "read line by line", no middle-score explanation.
- Score now and score after. Skip the three-bureau score mechanics.
- "Sometimes it's one item, sometimes it's twelve" is the way to handle variable file condition.
- Guarantee wording: if you're not happy with what you get, email us and we'll refund you. No window set yet.
- Close is one plain sentence about the soft inquiry. No stacked fragments.
- Proof: ten years, hundreds of files, tens of thousands of data points across the funding ecosystem, reverse engineered.
- Never say "the number" or "your number".
- Never name EIN, DUNS, net-30, gas cards.
- Cost of inaction gets said out loud: on a lot of files that spread is a couple hundred thousand dollars in funding you're missing out on.
- Say "missing out on", not "missed opportunity".
- "On a lot of files" rather than "typically", since typically reads as an average-results claim.
- Every ad points at maximum funding, even the ones that open on a decline.
- Second person, straight at them. Never "most business owners have no idea", always "you have no idea".
- The product is credit card stacking. Nobody is being handed a take-it-or-leave-it bank offer, so never frame it that way. The file either gets worked or it doesn't.
- Future pacing stays broad: starts your new business, funds the one you're already running, or gets you ahead of the guys you're competing with. Avoid "second location", too specific.
- Proof reads as full sentences. "I've been doing this for ten years. I've seen hundreds of files." Never the clipped "Ten years. Hundreds of files."
- State the situation, never hint at it. No riddles, no "which one you're sitting on".
- Strongest register is I will / you will. "I'll pull your credit. I'll show you where your score sits. I'll hand you the list." Chris speaking directly, doing the thing.
- Industry slams stay about the industry as a whole, never a named competitor.
- Never disqualify the clean-credit owner. He is the best ICP, because he wants maximum funding and most files need work whether the owner knows it or not. Write him in, never out.
- Pull people in on wanting the most their file can get, rather than on needing repair.

## HOUSE RULES ADDED ON THE HAYNES SET

- "10x your file" is approved language and it carries the middle of the ad.
- Never make the viewer feel stupid for not knowing this. No lines about what it is costing them relative to their own business decisions. Blame the industry, never the owner.
- Show the upside as what the capital does: scales the company to multiple six figures a month, funds the deal they have been waiting on, gets the new thing off the ground.
- Haynes ads do not lead with the price. The straight offer ads do, which is what filters before the click. That price difference is most of the CPC gap between the two sets.
- Haynes traffic is earlier, not lower quality. The landing page and VSL carry more weight on it.
- Never say the viewer "got closed". They may have hung up. Say the call turned into a pitch.
- The roadmap itself is the value. Say so out loud: the exact steps in the exact order are what separate a little funding from hundreds of thousands personal plus hundreds of thousands business across multiple businesses, potentially seven figures. Every company gatekeeps it for that reason.
- Always "potentially seven figures", never a flat seven figures.
